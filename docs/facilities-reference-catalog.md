# Facilities reference catalog

Reality-grounding corpus for the procedural circuit-facilities generator.
34 real circuits across six facility classes. Facts marked *(est.)* are
estimated from aerial imagery/OSM rather than published documentation.
No reference photographs are committed; source links only.

The catalog is a **prior over plausible design**, not a library to copy.

## Dimensional norms (documented)

- FIA Appendix O (2026): pit lane **≥ 12 m** total width; garages and race
  control adjacent to the start straight; pit wall / signalling platform
  separates lane from track. Entry/exit must not interfere with the racing
  line.
- F1 Sporting Regs (2026): **fast lane ≤ 3.5 m** (adjacent to pit wall);
  inner/working lane ≈ 8.5 m balance.
- Fuji garage bay module **7 m wide** (34 bays × 7 m × 22 m deep in the main
  building, 11 × 7 m × 11 m in the second) — the 6–8 m garage frontage
  rhythm recurs everywhere.
- Silverstone Wing: 41 garages over ~360–390 m of pit frontage.
- Bahrain: up to 36 garages; Le Mans: 60–62 (endurance scale).

## Modern permanent Grand Prix facilities

### Fuji Speedway (JP) — international, permanent, 2005 Tilke rebuild
- Pit lane on the inside of the 1.5 km main straight; two pit buildings
  (A: 34 × 7×22 m garages + Crystal Rooms upstairs; B: 11 × 7×11 m).
- Main grandstand 22,288 seats, two-tier, 10 VIP rooms, faces pit building;
  Turn-1 stand 2,200. Big supported roof screens on the pit building.
- Confidence: documented. Sources: fsw.tv/guide/facility/racing.html,
  motorsports.jaf.or.jp/course/circuit/fuji/

### Suzuka (JP) — international, permanent, 1962 Honda / rebuilt 1987+
- Pit building with repeated garage modules + upper hospitality; main stand
  opposite with full-length canopy; giant roof-mounted screens; pit lane
  between garage apron and pit wall. *(est. from imagery)*
- Confidence: mixed. Sources: suzukacircuit.jp, racingcircuits.info

### Bahrain International Circuit (BH) — international, permanent, 2004
- HOK architecture, Tilke track; Arabic/Bedouin-tent tensile shade canopies;
  36 garages with hospitality balconies; main stand ~10,500; Sakhir/VIP
  tower as vertical punctuation; strong night lighting for floodlit races.
- Confidence: documented. Sources: gulfconstructiononline.com (367343),
  bahrain media kit, cebarco.com.bh

### Sepang (MY) — international, permanent, 1999 Tilke
- Double-frontage pit complex (pits serve two straights back-to-back);
  sweeping double-tier main-stand roof (hibiscus-inspired), long canopy
  over the pit lane. *(est. details)*
- Confidence: mixed. Sources: sepangcircuit.com/architecture

### Silverstone (GB) — international, permanent, 2011 "Wing"
- Populous; ~380 m pit building, 41 garages, angular continuously-changing
  roofline ("flight"), metallic rainscreen + red mesh accent; race control,
  media, podium inside; hospitality above garages; main spectator seating in
  separate stands around the track (Luffield, Copse, Hangar…).
- Confidence: documented. Sources: populous.com/showcases/silverstone-circuit,
  ashandlacy.com case study

### Circuit of the Americas (US) — international, permanent, 2012
- Tilke race-operations buildings (limestone façade, low-slung, balconies);
  Miró Rivera main grandstand: exposed kit-of-parts steel, red tubular
  frames, white nested-tube canopy, cantilevered trusses; 251 ft red/white
  observation tower with double-helix stair as landmark punctuation.
- Confidence: documented. Sources: architecturalrecord.com 7553,
  mirorivera.com/cota-grandstand

### Yas Marina (AE) — international, permanent, 2009 Tilke
- Hotel straddling the circuit (grid-shell veil), harbour-side pit complex,
  covered grandstands on both sides of the pit straight; strong night race
  lighting. *(est. details)*
- Confidence: mixed. Sources: yasmarinacircuit.com, Wikipedia

### Shanghai (CN) — international, permanent, 2004 Tilke
- Monumental main grandstand with wing-like floating roof ("clouds"),
  lotus-inspired team buildings behind the pits on stilts over water.
  *(est. details)*
- Confidence: mixed. Sources: racingcircuits.info, Wikipedia

### Istanbul Park (TR) — international, permanent, 2005 Tilke
- Long low pit building, terraced main stand, extensive use of hillside

- Confidence: estimated. Sources: racingcircuits.info

### Paul Ricard (FR) — international, permanent, 1970 / modernized
- Functional low pit building, mistral-straight composition, striped runoff

### Red Bull Ring (AT) — international, permanent, 1969/1996/2011

## Historic and legacy facilities

### Monza (IT) — international, permanent, 1922
- Palimpsest: 1922 3,000-seat main stand + six 1,000-seat laterals (Vietti
  Violi, Liberty-influenced); 1989–90 two-storey pit building; long
  start/finish straight in a royal park; layered modernization eras visible.
- Confidence: documented. Sources: monzanet.it/en/history, FIA 2018 media kit

### Imola (IT) — international, permanent, 1953
- Hillside park circuit; pit complex rebuilt repeatedly; compact garages
  along the downhill pit straight; main stand opposite, valley setting.
  *(est.)*
- Confidence: estimated. Sources: autodromoimola.it, FIA media kits

### Interlagos (BR) — international, permanent, 1940
- Long pit lane + garage/paddock building on the uphill start straight;
  covered paddock structure (Effect Arquitetura); Sector B grandstand
  (Frederico Marques); repeated municipal modernizations.
- Confidence: documented. Sources: prefeitura.sp.gov.br (310317), effect.arq.br

### Spa-Francorchamps (BE) — international/endurance, permanent, 1921
- TWO pit complexes: F1 pits on the start straight + Endurance pit lane and
  garages on the La Source→Eau Rouge run; Endurance grandstand opposite the
  endurance garages with views La Source→Raidillon→Kemmel.
- Confidence: documented (grandstand); rest mixed. Sources:
  spa-francorchamps.be news 295/325

### Nürburgring GP (DE) — international, permanent, 1984
- Functional 1980s pit building, big main stand with canopy, Mercedes-Arena
  bowl at T1; Eifel terrain. *(est.)*
- Confidence: estimated. Sources: nuerburgring.de

### Goodwood (GB) — club/regional, permanent, 1948
- No pit building in the modern sense: low pit counters + open paddock
  shelters behind, period wooden structures, no grandstands — banked
  spectator viewing; heritage atmosphere preserved by policy. *(est.)*
- Confidence: estimated. Sources: goodwood.com

### Brands Hatch (GB) — national, permanent, 1926/1950s
- Natural bowl: pit straight at the foot of Paddock Hill; compact pit
  building; small stands terraced into the amphitheater slopes. *(est.)*
- Confidence: estimated. Sources: brandshatch.co.uk

### Zandvoort (NL) — international, permanent, 1948
- Pit Grandstand opposite the garages + renovated Main Grandstand on the
  same straight; compact dune-site footprint; temporary Nussli overlay
  structures for F1 crowd scale.
- Confidence: documented. Sources: circuitzandvoort.nl, nussli.com

  Bugatti layout and the 24h; big main stands opposite; Dunlop bridge/curve
  landmarks; extensive night-race lighting on the pit straight.
- Confidence: documented. Sources: 24h-lemans.com (62 garages),
  lemans-tourisme.com brochure

### Daytona (US) — endurance/national, permanent, 1959
- Pit road + garages on the frontstretch inside the tri-oval; massive
  100k+ seat grandstand outside the banking; infield hospitality patios at
  pit road; stadium logic, not road-course logic. *(est. details)*
- Confidence: documented. Sources: daytonainternationalspeedway.com

### Mount Panorama, Bathurst (AU) — national/endurance, permanent, 1938

  viewing mostly on open slopes.
- Confidence: documented. Sources: bathurst.nsw.gov.au, v8sleuth.com.au

### Road America (US) — national, permanent, 1955

### Watkins Glen (US) — national, permanent, 1956

### Laguna Seca (US) — national, permanent, 1957

## Temporary and street circuits

### Monaco (MC) — temporary-street, 1929
- Harbor-side pit lane (Port Hercules) with compact permanent-ish garages
  inserted into the quay; everything else temporary: stands over the
  harbor, barriers, fencing; pit lane narrow and constrained; assembled and
  dismantled per event (2026 regulations cover the montage/démontage).
- Confidence: documented. Sources: legimonaco.mc, FIA event notes

### Singapore Marina Bay (SG) — temporary-street, 2008
- PERMANENT F1 Pit Building (3-storey, garage + hospitality, year-round
  venue) inside an otherwise fully temporary Circuit Park: Pit / Super Pit

  floodlighting system on masts.
- Confidence: documented. Sources: singaporegp.sg, stb.gov.sg

### Jeddah (SA) — temporary-street (hybrid), 2021

## Recurring relationships (the grammar prior)

1. **Pit building ↔ main grandstand face each other across the pit
   straight** (Fuji, Suzuka, Bahrain, Zandvoort, Interlagos, Le Mans).
   The straight is a stage set: stand | fence | track | pit wall | lane |
   apron | building | paddock.
2. **Garage module rhythm 6–8 m frontage**, one door per bay, doors face the
   apron; rear service side faces the paddock, never the lane.
3. **Upper floors over garages** = hospitality/race control (Fuji Crystal
   Rooms, Bahrain balconies, Silverstone Wing) — balconies overlook the pit
   lane.
4. **One vertical punctuation** per complex: control/timing tower (COTA
   251 ft, Bahrain Sakhir tower, Suzuka screens) — usually near start/finish.
5. **Roof type correlates with climate**: desert → tensile/canopy shade
   (Bahrain); wet/temperate → full cantilever roofs (Suzuka, Silverstone);
   club → none or minimal.
6. **Endurance = longer pit complex** (Le Mans 60+ garages ≈ 1.5–2× F1).
7. **Temporary street circuits**: modular everything — scaffolding decks,
   fabric roofs, portable barriers; at most one permanent anchor building
   (Singapore pit building, Vegas plaza).
8. **Club circuits**: no grandstand logic; clubhouse-as-viewing; premium
   materials over capacity.
9. **Hillside circuits use terrain as the grandstand** (Red Bull Ring,
   Bathurst, Brands Hatch, Road America): terraces/banks, few structures.
10. **Historic circuits show layered eras** — original low structures plus
    obvious later modernization (Monza palimpsest).
11. **Screens are supported towers**, oriented toward the biggest crowd
    concentration, near start/finish or big viewing corners.
12. **Pit entry leaves before/at the final corner; pit exit rejoins after
    the pit area**, never onto the racing line mid-corner (FIA App. O).
